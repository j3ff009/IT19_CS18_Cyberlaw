01 Central idea

Capability is not authority

Technical capability does not create legal authority. Responsible computing requires authorization, lawful purpose, ethical judgment, and reliable evidence.

Can I?

A technical question about what a tool or account permits.

May I?

A legal and organizational question about authorization.

Should I?

An ethical and professional question about rights and harm.

02 Digital society

Related—but not interchangeable

Cybercrime

Unlawful conduct involving computers, networks, digital devices, data, or online services.

Cybersecurity

Protection of systems and information, plus the management of digital risk and incidents.

Cyberlaw

The wider body of laws and legal principles governing digital systems, information, transactions, and conduct.

Key distinction: Not every security incident is a crime. An accidental failure may affect security; deliberate unauthorized conduct may also create criminal or disciplinary responsibility.

03 Classification

Start with the conduct

Cyber-dependent

The offense fundamentally depends on a computer system.

Illegal accessMalwareData interferenceDDoS
VS

Cyber-enabled

Technology expands traditional wrongdoing through speed, scale, reach, or anonymity.

FraudImpersonationHarassmentScams

Technology may be the target, the tool, the environment, or the source of evidence.

CIA Harm lens

What security property was harmed?

C

Confidentiality

Information is available only to authorized persons. Copying a customer database without right violates confidentiality.

I

Integrity

Information remains accurate and changes only through authorized action. Changing a grade harms integrity.

A

Availability

Systems remain accessible to legitimate users. Flooding a school server until it fails harms availability.

04 Cybercrime Prevention Act

Republic Act No. 10175

The principal Philippine statute defining and penalizing several offenses involving computer systems and data.

System & data offenses

Illegal access, interception, data interference, system interference, misuse of devices, and cybersquatting.

Computer-related offenses

Forgery, fraud, and identity theft involving computer data or systems.

Content-related offenses

Includes cyberlibel, subject to constitutional review and judicial interpretation.

A computer’s involvement is not a complete legal conclusion. Identify the exact act, facts, authority, intent, harm, and relevant jurisprudence.

4.1 RA 10175

Illegal access & interception

Illegal accessIntentionally accessing all or part of a computer system without right. Knowing a password or discovering a vulnerability does not create permission.
Illegal interceptionUnauthorized technical interception of non-public transmissions of computer data.

Authorized test

A written penetration-testing agreement defines target, time, methods, limits, data handling, and reporting.

≠

Unauthorized proof

An outsider enters a database “only to prove” a weakness. Technical similarity does not erase the difference in authority.

4.2 RA 10175

Data, systems, devices & domains

Data interferenceUnauthorized alteration, damage, deletion, deterioration, or suppression of computer data.
System interferenceHindering the functioning or availability of a computer or network through covered unauthorized acts.
Misuse of devicesSpecified handling of tools, programs, passwords, or access information intended for committing cybercrime; purpose and authorization matter.
CybersquattingBad-faith acquisition of a domain name under conditions defined by law, such as misleading users or improperly profiting from a protected identity.
4.3 RA 10175

Computer-related offenses

Forgery

Manipulating computer data so inauthentic information is treated as authentic—such as altering a digital receipt.

Fraud

Dishonest manipulation of data or systems that causes damage, such as deceptive electronic transfers.

Identity theft

Covered unauthorized handling or misuse of another person’s identifying information.

Overlap is possible: A fake profile using stolen credentials to request money may raise identity-theft, fraud, privacy, and other issues.

4.4 Court interpretation

Statutes must be read with decisions

RA 10175 covers libel committed through a computer system. In Disini v. Secretary of Justice, the Supreme Court upheld the provision as applied to the original author while rejecting its application to people who merely received and reacted to the post in the manner considered by the case.

Legal reasoning rule: Check whether a provision has been interpreted, amended, repealed, or declared unconstitutional. Original wording may not show its present legal effect.

05 Trust as an attack surface

Phishing manipulates decisions

An attacker impersonates a trusted person or organization to obtain credentials, OTPs, financial details, money, or another useful action.

Urgency

“Act in 30 minutes.”

Authority

“This is your bank.”

Fear

“Your account will close.”

Deception

A link opens a convincing fake page.

Practical warning: Never share an OTP, PIN, password, authentication code, or banking credential through an unexpected message.

AFASA Financial accounts

Republic Act No. 12010

The Anti-Financial Account Scamming Act addresses covered money-muling activities and social-engineering schemes involving financial accounts.

Money mule

A person uses, lends, sells, rents, opens, or allows the use of an account to receive, move, withdraw, or transfer known proceeds under circumstances covered by law.

Covered institutions

The law also places responsibilities on covered institutions to maintain adequate risk-management systems and controls.

Never lend or rent a bank or e-wallet account for suspicious transfers, even for a promised commission.

06 Malicious software

Behavior matters more than the label

VIRUS

Attaches

Spreads when an infected host file or program executes.

WORM

Self-spreads

Can propagate automatically across systems.

TROJAN

Disguises

Hides harmful behavior inside apparently legitimate software.

SPYWARE

Collects

Secretly gathers information.

RANSOMWARE

Blocks

Encrypts or prevents access and demands payment.

IMPACT

Overlaps

May affect confidentiality, integrity, availability, privacy, and business continuity.

DPA Republic Act No. 10173

Privacy is a design concern

Transparency

People should understand how and why their information is collected and used.

Legitimate purpose

Processing must connect to a specified and lawful purpose.

Proportionality

Use information that is adequate, relevant, necessary, suitable, and not excessive.

Personal Information ControllerDetermines why and how personal information is processed.
Personal Information ProcessorProcesses personal information on behalf of a controller.
7.2 Privacy rights

Rights, incidents & notification

Be informedAccessObjectRectificationErasure or blockingComplaintDamagesData portability

A personal data breach may involve loss, unauthorized disclosure, acquisition, alteration, or access. A stolen database can create separate cybersecurity, privacy, cybercrime, organizational, and notification questions.

Under applicable NPC rules, qualifying breaches must be reported to the Commission and affected data subjects within 72 hours after knowledge or reasonable belief. Conditions and circumstances still matter.

08 Digital form & protected works

Recognition is not automatic proof

RA 8792

Electronic Commerce Act

Recognizes electronic transactions, data messages, documents, and signatures when applicable requirements are satisfied. Reliability, authentication, attribution, integrity, and context still matter.

RA 8293

Intellectual Property Code

Protects covered software and creative works. Owning a copy is not the same as owning copyright; a license defines many permitted uses.

Search-engine availability does not make material free to copy, modify, or distribute.

09 Online conduct

Other laws may be central

RA 9995

Intimate images

Addresses specified unauthorized capture, copying, distribution, publication, or exhibition. Consent to recording does not automatically mean consent to share.

RA 11313

Safe Spaces Act

Covers specified gender-based sexual harassment in online and other environments, plus institutional duties.

RA 11930

Protection of children

Addresses online sexual abuse or exploitation of children and exploitative materials.

RA 11934

SIM Registration

Requires registration before activation and sets rules for registration information and misuse.

RA 12010

Financial scams

Addresses specified money-muling and financial-account social engineering.

ACTLE Repeatable analysis

Turn an incident into structured reasoning

AAct

What exactly did the person do?

CContext

Permission, ownership, intent, victim, money, child, or testing scope?

TTechnology

Target, tool, medium, storage, or evidence?

LLaw

Which frameworks may apply or overlap?

EEvidence

What proves each important fact?

Replace vague claims like “someone hacked a system” with observable acts: entered, copied, altered, deleted, deceived, impersonated, transferred, or distributed.

11 Evidence quality

A screenshot is a lead—not automatic proof

Authenticity

Is it genuine and what it claims to be?

Integrity

Was it preserved complete and unaltered?

Reliability

Can the system and collection method be trusted?

Relevance

Does it help prove or disprove an issue?

Possible evidence includes original emails and messages, access and server logs, timestamps, transaction references, audit logs, files and metadata, URLs, media, digital receipts, and device records.

11.2 Evidence handling

Document every handoff

1Identify

Locate potential evidence lawfully.

2Collect

Record when, where, and by whom.

3Secure

Protect originals and control access.

4Examine

Use reliable methods and document changes.

5Present

Explain custody, integrity, and context.

Victims should preserve original messages, transaction references, emails, URLs, account details, dates, and times. Investigations remain subject to lawful authority, privacy, due process, and prescribed procedures.

12 Apply A-C-T-L-E

Cases: access, fraud & privacy

Grade modification

A student uses a professor’s password and changes 74 to 94. Consider illegal access, data interference, authorization, logs, timestamps, and audit history.

Fake bank website

A fake alert captures credentials and an OTP before a PHP 40,000 transfer. Consider deception, RA 12010, RA 10175, the original message, URL, logs, and transaction trail.

Customer database

An administrator copies 50,000 records and sells them. Consider privacy, cybercrime, confidentiality, employment duties, file-transfer records, devices, and communications.

12.2 Apply A-C-T-L-E

Cases: consent, money & testing

Private video

Two adults agree to record, but one later uploads the file without the other’s permission. Recording consent does not automatically authorize distribution.

Money mule

A person knowingly forwards scam proceeds for 10%. Examine knowledge, account control, transaction patterns, messages, benefit, and RA 12010.

“Ethical” hacking

One student reports a weakness; another enters the database to prove it. Good intention and no theft do not create authorization.

13 Trusted access

Privilege creates responsibility

Legal

Does the act violate law, contract, or enforceable duty?

Ethical

Does it respect rights and avoid unjustified harm?

Professional

Does it meet the standard expected of a trusted specialist?

Organizational

Does it follow approved roles, policies, and purposes?

Least privilege: Give only the access needed for legitimate duties; time-limit, log, review, and remove access when roles change.

14 Shared responsibility

No single control is enough

Technology

Authentication, encryption, backups, segmentation, monitoring, secure configuration.

People

Awareness, verification, ethical conduct, and timely reporting.

Policy

Roles, acceptable use, access approval, incident response, evidence handling.

Law

Rights, prohibited conduct, remedies, duties, and investigative procedure.

Design choices create legal consequences: what data to collect, which code to use, who receives privilege, what to log, and how incidents are escalated.

✓ Key takeaways

Begin with act and authorization

  • Cyberlaw is broader than cybercrime and cybersecurity.
  • Cyber-dependent and cyber-enabled offenses use technology differently.
  • The CIA triad identifies confidentiality, integrity, and availability harms.
  • RA 10175 is central but other laws may overlap.
  • Authorization, purpose, intent, context, and harm change legal meaning.
  • Digital evidence must be authentic, preserved, documented, and lawfully collected.
  • A-C-T-L-E structures analysis of unfamiliar incidents.
  • Technical capability creates responsibility—not permission.
